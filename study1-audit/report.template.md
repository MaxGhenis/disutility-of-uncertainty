# Can Study 1 identify stable individual marginal-tax beliefs?

**Verdict:** the archive supports reproducible individual *forecast projections* and conditional measurement-model diagnostics. It does **not** separately identify stable individual marginal-tax beliefs, their noise-free variance, or a national welfare input. The main obstacles are very short and uneven local income support, influential observations, processing-sensitive tails, and the absence of independent repeated measurements. There is useful within-survey consistency over broad income ranges; interpreting it as stable local belief heterogeneity requires additional restrictions.

The actual source is **Alex Rees-Jones and Dmitry Taubinsky, “Measuring ‘Schmeduling’,” Review of Economic Studies 87(5), 2399–2438 (2020), DOI 10.1093/restud/rdz045**. The names in the assignment were informal. This audit uses the retained [article](https://alexreesjones.github.io/papers/Measuring%20Schmeduling.pdf), [online appendix](https://alexreesjones.github.io/papers/Measuring%20Schmeduling%20Online%20Appendix.pdf), and [author-linked replication archive](https://www.dropbox.com/s/2q47bj8arbcgr3i/Schmeduling%20Replication%20Code.zip?dl=1). It is separate from completed Disutility PR3; no welfare inputs or implementation files changed.

For each respondent the audit fits forecast on income with an intercept and subtracts the same income projection of stored true tax. Thus “error” below is a **signed finite-design slope error**, measured in percentage points. It is a point-MTR error only with additional local-affinity and measurement assumptions. All SDs use equal respondent mass and denominator N; no person or survey weights are inferred.

**A finite-sample falsification test.** For a proposed dollar cap a, require that some intercept and slope satisfy every inequality `|forecast_j − intercept − slope × income_j| ≤ a`. Intersecting all pairwise slope intervals gives the exact feasible slope set. A separate minimax calculation finds the smallest cap for any line; tests verify agreement. This test permits arbitrary correlation of bounded errors and no restriction to slopes in [0,1].

The audit also supplies conditional, deterministic bounds for the bias, RMSE and SD of latent **projection** errors. If every answer's error is at most a dollars, individual slope error is at most `a × sum(abs(income − mean income))/Sxx`; an RMS dollar cap gives `a × sqrt(n/Sxx)`. No independence, zero-bias or uncorrelated-noise assumption is needed. At a=$100, the local pair's median radius is **{{pair_radius}} pp**, versus **{{selected_radius}} pp** for the 2,470-person screened sample. These are assumption-indexed sensitivities, not estimated noise caps or confidence intervals. The latter sample's SD outer bounds are {{selected_sd_bounds}} pp under that cap; treating them as stable MTR dispersion would contradict the preceding affine-model failure for most of the sample. Without a noise restriction, no finite latent upper bound follows. [Full bounds](results/finite_sample_bounds.csv).

**Repeated measures and correlated error.** There is only one incidental exact-income repeat, for one person; its forecasts differ by $401. There are no independent waves in these files. Splitting each person's income-sorted points into alternating subsets creates two disjoint forecast projections, conditional on enough distinct points. A second split by qnum parity is retained as a specification sensitivity. Neither is a test–retest design. The own-income pair alone cannot be divided into two slope measurements.

{{repeat_table}}

Covariances and jackknife SEs above are in squared percentage points. The SE is a sampling diagnostic under independent respondents, not a measurement-error correction. Under the additional model `s_A=theta+u_A`, `s_B=theta+u_B`, both errors orthogonal to the *same* theta and to each other, the covariance estimates Var(theta). The two subsets need not target the same theta when beliefs are curved or slope errors depend on income support. The broad-range correlation of {{broad_corr}} is evidence of within-survey response consistency, but every broad-range true-tax schedule is nonlinear on the observed support.

Even granting a common target, correlated error changes the interpretation. For the 1,986-person local split, the zero-error-correlation model implies an SD of {{naive_sd}} pp. Allowing error correlation of **approximately {{half_rho}}** lowers it to **{{half_sd}} pp**; setting error correlation to the observed correlation (**approximately {{zero_rho}}**) permits **zero** common-target variance at exactly the same observed second moments. This sensitivity assumes errors remain orthogonal to the latent target and is conditional on the sample moments; it is not an estimate of the actual error correlation. [Sensitivity table](results/correlated_error_sensitivity.csv).

An exact factorial **synthetic counterexample** independently validates the failure: homogeneous stable slopes plus a respondent-specific session response slope of SD 10 pp produce positive split covariance despite zero stable latent variance. A common dollar intercept cancels, so correlation alone is not sufficient; its alignment with income matters. All synthetic outputs are explicitly labeled and separated from empirical results. [Counterexample and assumptions](results/SYNTHETIC_correlated_error.csv).

**Feasible next measurement.** Prioritize a modest two-session pilot with identical target schedules, randomized question order, no intervening feedback, and six or eight prespecified income points per session. Verify the complete tax function on each interval before interpreting any slope as an MTR. Use a separate monetary-choice measurement to test whether survey slopes predict decisions. Vary elicitation format independently across sessions to measure common-method persistence, preserve raw answers and timings, and preregister handling of curved beliefs and bounded-error failures. Keep narrow-bracket respondents as a reported stratum rather than silently excluding them. Under an explicitly assumed $500 iid error SD, six evenly spaced points over $30,000 yield a one-session slope SE of {{design_se}} pp, compared with {{pair_design_se}} pp for a $500 pair. More same-session points cannot remove a session-level response slope. [Concrete pilot and go/no-go criteria](MEASUREMENT_PROTOCOL.md); [prospective arithmetic](results/prospective_design.csv).

**Scope and reproducibility.** All calculations are local and deterministic; no Stata code is executed. The study concerns a hypothetical 2014 federal salary-tax filer, with standard deduction and no additional credits/deductions in the prompt. Panel recruitment approximating some census demographics does not establish representative latent belief variance. These results do not cover state/payroll taxes, benefits, comprehensive household incentives or welfare inputs. The published structural grid shares and average ironing coefficient are not substituted for an identified type distribution. [Article pp. 2409–2411 and 2420](https://alexreesjones.github.io/papers/Measuring%20Schmeduling.pdf).
