# Prioritized next measurement

The binding experiment is an **independent test–retest plus a change in elicitation method**, not a larger sample of the existing 16-question survey. This is an offline protocol proposal. No recruitment, spending, author contact or compute job has been launched.

1. **Prepare auditable schedules and raw measurement.** Use the same hypothetical federal salary-tax scope as Study 1. Before fielding, obtain and verify the original liability-generation/cleaning code or construct and independently validate a complete schedule from the appropriate primary tax source. Record component scope, units, deductions, phaseouts and breakpoint ownership. Do not label an interval affine from a bracket code or observed grid alone. If that cannot be done, pilot on explicitly assigned experimental schedules whose complete formulas are specified; report external-validity limits. Preserve the untouched survey export, actual presentation order, income draws, answers before and after every transformation, timestamps and all exclusions.

2. **Pilot approximately 100 respondents across two sessions, 7–14 days apart**, with six to eight prespecified income points per session. This N is an operational pilot proposal, not a power claim. Keep the same household/schedule target across sessions, randomize order, and delay accuracy feedback until both sessions finish. Choose the widest income interval that has a verified constant incremental rate and a meaningful local interpretation. Counterbalance points on both sides of the anchor when feasible. Retain narrow-interval cases as their own stratum, and report their achieved precision. Never extrapolate a wide-span result to them.

3. **Cross the session with measurement format.** Randomize the order of total-liability elicitation versus explicit incremental-liability elicitation, and include a separately incentivized taxable/untaxed monetary-choice task on the same schedule. Independent reordering alone cannot distinguish a durable response heuristic from a belief; changing format and comparing choices are stronger tests. The choice interpretation still needs explicit preference, indifference, attention and noise assumptions. Check the choice task's information provision does not teach the subsequent survey answers. A distinct behavioral validation session or randomized ordering can expose that contamination.

4. **Preregister falsifiable estimands and checks.** Primary estimand: covariance of locally valid slope-error measures across sessions within a defined respondent stratum. Secondary: cross-format covariance and predictive validity for monetary choices. Keep signed bias separate from dispersion. Report selection, attrition, income spans, leverage, local-line specification failures and all cap sensitivities. Use held-out observations to test affine beliefs. Fit a crossed person/session/method error model only with enough repeated cells and state its zero-covariance restrictions; two measurements alone cannot identify unrestricted correlated errors. Test how common-method and person-by-session shocks alter conclusions.

5. **Expand only after the pilot meets prespecified criteria.** A plausible proposed gate is that at least 80% of each predeclared stratum supplies two identifiable slopes, median achieved per-session precision is within a prespecified scientifically useful range (e.g. 5 pp under a calibrated measurement model), and cross-session/cross-format covariance remains distinguishable from zero after sampling and correlated-error sensitivities. These thresholds are design proposals, not findings or evidence that any current sample meets them. Report and redesign around failures; do not silently drop the difficult strata. Estimate sample size for the confirmatory study from the pilot's variance components and target precision, with attrition and multiple comparisons handled explicitly.

The [prospective arithmetic table](results/prospective_design.csv) isolates a useful design tradeoff. With **assumed** independent dollar errors of SD $500, a two-point $500 span gives a slope SE of 141.4 pp. Six evenly spaced points spanning $30,000 give 1.99 pp, or 1.41 pp for the mean of two independent sessions. A $30,000 constant-rate interval may not exist near everyone's own income. All calculations are prospective and assume a correct line; they do not remove shared session or method slopes. More points and a wider interval solve different problems from an independent remeasurement.

A source-code recovery request could close the tax-construction and processing gaps without new fieldwork, but it would not create independent repetitions of beliefs. Any outreach or recruitment requires the separate authorization applicable to that project.
